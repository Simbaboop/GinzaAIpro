# ADR-0017: Canonical Evidence Semantics

## Status

Accepted.

Normative beginning with Sprint 2.4A.

## Purpose

Define the canonical semantic layer between validated Evidence and
operational Intelligence.

This ADR establishes that Intelligence must reason over structured semantic
representations rather than directly over unstructured Evidence.

## Background

This decision builds on:

- ADR-0015: Canonical Capture Boundary;
- ADR-0016: Canonical Business Diagnosis;
- SAS-0002: Capture Engine;
- SAS-0003: Intelligence Engine.

ADR-0015 and SAS-0002 establish the canonical path from external reality
through `BusinessSignal` capture and Evidence qualification. ADR-0016 and
SAS-0003 establish Intelligence as the canonical operational interpretation
of validated Evidence.

Sprint 2.5 implementation discovered a structural ambiguity between those
boundaries: validated Evidence currently preserves factual correctness,
provenance, and traceability, but it does not preserve sufficient structured
semantics to support deterministic operational interpretation. Its factual
meaning cannot be consumed reliably by governed interpretation policies
without either interpreting unstructured language directly or introducing
unstated architectural assumptions.

The implementation was correctly halted rather than embedding those
assumptions in code. A distinct canonical semantic layer is required before
deterministic Intelligence can be implemented.

## Canonical decision

The following doctrine is normative:

> Evidence establishes what has been validated.

> Evidence Semantics establishes the structured meaning contained within
> validated Evidence.

> Intelligence establishes the operational meaning of those semantic facts.

Operational Intelligence shall never interpret raw language directly.

Evidence Semantics is the sole canonical boundary through which factual
meaning passes from validated Evidence into operational Intelligence.

## Governing question

Evidence asks:

> "What facts have been validated?"

Evidence Semantics asks:

> "What structured facts do those validated facts express?"

Intelligence asks:

> "What operational meaning emerges from those structured facts?"

These questions are distinct. Answering one must not absorb or repeat the
responsibility of another layer.

## Canonical pipeline

```text
Reality
      │
BusinessSignal
      │
Evidence
      │
Evidence Semantics
      │
Intelligence
      │
Analytical Views
      │
Priority
      │
Recommendation
      │
Execution
      │
Outcome
```

Each layer transforms information exactly once. No downstream layer may
repeat an upstream responsibility.

In particular, Evidence Semantics structures the meaning already present in
validated facts; it does not diagnose the business. Intelligence interprets
those canonical semantic facts operationally; it does not repeat validation
or semantic extraction.

## Responsibilities

### Evidence

Evidence is responsible for:

- immutable validated fact;
- provenance;
- traceability.

### Evidence Semantics

Evidence Semantics is responsible for:

- structured semantic representation;
- normalization;
- deterministic extraction;
- semantic lineage.

Semantic lineage must preserve attribution to the validated Evidence from
which each canonical semantic representation was derived.

### Intelligence

Intelligence is responsible for:

- operational interpretation;
- governed reasoning;
- diagnosis.

Intelligence consumes canonical Evidence Semantics and applies deterministic
interpretation policies. It does not extract meaning directly from
unstructured Evidence.

## Architectural rules

The following rules are mandatory:

1. Evidence shall not contain business diagnosis.
2. Evidence Semantics shall not recommend action.
3. Intelligence shall not reinterpret raw language.
4. Recommendation shall not redefine Intelligence.
5. Execution shall not redefine Recommendation.
6. Evidence Semantics must remain attributable to validated Evidence.
7. Intelligence must consume canonical semantic representations rather than
   unstructured language.
8. No downstream layer may repeat validation, semantic extraction, or
   interpretation already owned by an upstream layer.

## AI boundary

LLMs, OCR, speech recognition, parsers, and similar systems may assist in
producing Evidence Semantics.

No AI system shall produce canonical operational Intelligence directly.
Operational Intelligence must remain governed by deterministic
interpretation policies.

Any assisted semantic-production process remains subject to the canonical
Evidence Semantics boundary. Technology-specific or probabilistic output
does not become operational Intelligence merely because it was generated by
an AI system.

## Consequences

This decision:

- enables deterministic operational reasoning over structured inputs;
- improves explainability through explicit semantic lineage;
- establishes a governed AI boundary between assisted extraction and
  canonical operational interpretation;
- supports future extensibility without coupling Intelligence to raw
  language formats;
- strengthens separation of concerns among validation, semantic extraction,
  and diagnosis;
- enables reusable semantic extraction engines;
- allows multiple ingestion pipelines to converge on one canonical semantic
  boundary;
- improves COSMOS compatibility through explicit semantic separation;
- requires Evidence Semantics to be defined before Sprint 2.5 Intelligence
  implementation resumes;
- prevents operational interpretation policies from depending on opaque
  natural-language parsing.

## Relationship to HCES

This ADR introduces the HCES principle of **Semantic Separation**:

> Operational reasoning shall never depend directly upon unstructured
> language.

All operational reasoning must consume canonical semantic representations.
This principle applies regardless of whether the upstream meaning was
extracted by deterministic parsers, AI-assisted systems, or another governed
mechanism.

## Out of scope

This ADR intentionally does not define:

- semantic entities;
- semantic extraction;
- semantic normalization;
- semantic confidence;
- implementation.

Those decisions belong to SAS-0002A.
